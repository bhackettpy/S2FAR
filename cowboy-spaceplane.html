Cowboy Spaceplane: A Turbojet–Rocket Flight Research Demonstrator

The Cowboy Spaceplane Technology Demonstrator is a proposed subscale unmanned aircraft research effort combining microturbojet propulsion with solid rocket augmentation. Led by Dr. Paul Elliott with Dr. Kurt Rouser and Dr. Anthony Comer, the project brings together OSU expertise in rocket propulsion, turbine engines, flight dynamics, and controls. Its purpose is to study propulsion integration and flight transitions relevant to future horizontal-launch spaceplane concepts.

The planned effort uses two modified composite jet airframes and a progressive ground- and flight-test campaign. The first aircraft supports initial integration and transition evaluations, while the second supports later demonstrations after the earlier results have been assessed. S2FAR will provide simulation, control-law design, stability analysis, and flight-test operations support. Comparing measured behavior with simulation predictions will help characterize the transition dynamics.

Expected outputs include flight and ground-test datasets, validated simulation models, technical reports, and a conference paper. Testing is planned at OSU's Stillwater test field, with later demonstrations at the Oklahoma Air and Space Port at Burns Flat in coordination with the Oklahoma Space Industry Development Authority. The work will also train graduate and undergraduate researchers and provide preliminary results for future external proposals.
